Did You Overpay?

One hour with Dana Samuelson, FBI expert witness on gold fraud, and a Dallas attorney who goes after predatory pricing in precious metals. An educational event. The invite goes to your list only. Target date Thursday 10 December.

This document itemizes the timeline, what each party gets out of it, what a yes sets in motion, and three things that would stop it.

Prepared for Dana Samuelson Cynthia Dalton, LeadGrow 18 November 2026

The ask

A private educational event for your list

You said you did not think he would do it. That is probably because the version he is thinking about is publicity, and publicity might create competition for him.

So do not offer him publicity. Offer him new business, and awareness of what he does and the expertise behind it.

Would you spend an hour with my email list, privately, explaining how predatory pricing works and how someone would know if it happened to them?

Let him know there will be no recording released outside this list, and that we are not promoting it on social media. His name can be withheld from the invite if he would rather, although including it works in his favor, because it puts his expertise in front of the list. He can read the invite before it goes.

That is a different question from the one he already declined. He is not being asked to advertise. He is being asked to spend an hour in a closed room with people who match his client profile, assembled by someone who has already sent him business.

You might mention that you have referred clients to him before. That makes this a continuation of something that works and brings him value, not a favor.

What each party gets

Why this works for everyone in the room

The attorney

An audience that is his buyer profile, gathered by someone they already trust. The referral record is established rather than theoretical. No advertising cost, and no competitive signal, because nothing leaves the list.

Costs him: 60 minutes live, plus a 20 minute prep call.

You and AGE

Something no competitor in the category is offering. It positions you as the honest operator by contrast rather than by assertion. It also surfaces the subscribers who were overcharged somewhere else and do not know it yet. One already found you on his own, after paying for roughly half the metal he should have received.

Costs you: one send sequence, one hour live, and the hosting time.

Your buying clients

Learning how predatory pricing actually works, how to tell whether it happened to them, what recourse exists, and who to call. For anyone it has happened to, this is the most useful hour they will get from a metals dealer this year.

Costs them: an hour, and only if they choose to attend.

The subscribers who have never bought

The same hour. These are the people who read your emails and have never placed an order. Being served before being sold to is the entire argument for running this as education rather than promotion.

Costs them: an hour.

Cynthia

A repeatable interview format for the evergreen lane, tested on a topic that is already warm. It also forces the first real end to end test of the evaluation page, which currently has no working path from an appearance to a captured lead.

Costs me: the build, the copy, and the send sequence.

What stops this

Ordered by what is most likely to kill the date

  • The email systemBlocking

    Four sends sit in this plan and you cannot send to your own list without your partner present. The November replay stalled for this reason, and the webinar itself probably had lower attendance because of it. Bill leaves on 1 January.

    Get it in writing, before he is given a date, who is sending on 1, 7, 9 and 14 December. If nobody can commit to all four, the date moves.

  • The platformBlocking

    StreamYard collected registrants for the Jeff Snyder webinar and then delivered the stream to every connected platform except them. Not one registrant got in. It was painful to operate on top of that.

    Run it on Zoom. Registrant handling is what Zoom is built for, you already have it, and it removes the thing that broke last time. Milestone 2 locks this in writing.

  • He says noMilestone 1

    His stated objection is competition, and your own prediction is that he declines.

    Lead with what is not happening. The ask is an hour with a private list, not publicity, and what he gets is new business.

  • The form, and Joe25 Nov

    The registration form has to be wired before Thanksgiving, and Joe is the only person who can do it. Either he builds it or I hand him the spec and he implements it.

    Spec to Joe on the 24th so he has a clear day. He responds well to concise technical requests, so send the what and the why and nothing else.

  • Your expert witness casePrep call

    You are the expert witness in a case with trial now looking like 1 February in Dallas, six and a half years in. Nothing here can be allowed to jeopardize it.

    Agree the boundary in the prep call and write it into the host script. Your case is not the subject. His practice is.

  • Holiday attentionAccepted

    10 December beats the worst of it, but the list is thinner in December than it will be in January.

    Accept it. The replay carries the value, and the topic does not expire.

  • AttributionIn progress

    There is no working path today from an appearance to a captured lead. The evaluation page exists, is not linked in the menu, and asks people to send an email rather than fill in a form.

    It has to be fixed for this anyway, so let this event pay for the fix.

The new website is not a factor here. Registration goes on the old site, where Joe has already shipped once, so none of this waits on the launch.

The one date that cannot slip is Milestone 1. If his answer comes after Monday 23 November, your approval moves to the 30th and the first send still goes out on the 1st. You lose the buffer, not the date.
